Why do so many people hate Christmas shopping?

Ask around and the answers are wonderfully boring. Nobody hates the giving. They hate the logistics:

  • The crowds. The National Retail Federation expects 182.7 million consumers to shop during Thanksgiving weekend this year. Black Friday is the most popular day, with 69% (126 million) of those shoppers planning to shop, and 67% of them likely to shop in stores.
  • The lines. In-store shopping ends at a register, usually on the same day everyone else picked.
  • Too many choices. One search for "gifts for dad" and you're 40 tabs deep, comparing three versions of the same thing.
  • Not knowing what to get. The person who "doesn't need anything." The teenager. The in-law.
  • The money math. Twelve people, one budget, and no idea whether the total still fits.

You don't hate Christmas. You hate the errand version of it.

The good news: almost every one of those is a logistics problem, and logistics can be fixed.

How to make Christmas shopping easier: the couch plan

Five steps, in this order. The order matters, because each step makes the next one smaller.

1. Write one number before you look at a single gift

One total for everyone, written down. Then split it per person. If you want help with the split, our guide to how much to spend on Christmas gifts has a free calculator. For a full budget reset, see Christmas on a budget.

2. Shrink the list (kindly)

Fewer people means fewer decisions. For adults, a name draw works: everyone buys one good gift instead of six small ones. If your group goes that way, we have Secret Santa gifts under $25 ready.

3. Get everyone's wish list into one place

Texts, screenshots, a fridge note, a voice memo from Grandma: pull it all onto one page, one row per person. Our free Christmas list template prints.

4. One person at a time, one pick each

Open their list, pick the best thing that fits their cap, close the tab, move on. For kids, the 4 gift rule (want, need, wear, read) gives you four clear slots instead of an endless pile.

5. Order from home, with the shipping dates in mind

For delivery before Dec 25 in the lower 48, USPS recommends sending by Thu Dec 17 (Ground Advantage and First-Class Mail), Fri Dec 18 (Priority Mail) or Sat Dec 19 (Priority Mail Express), per its 2026 holiday shipping page. For anyone out of town, ship straight to them and skip the post office line too.

What to get when you don't know what to get

This is the part people who hate Christmas shopping hate most, and the fix is the least glamorous one: ask. Copy this and send it to the family chat:

"Doing my Christmas shopping from home this year. Send me 3 to 5 things you'd actually like, any price, by Sunday. Links or photos are perfect."

No reply from someone? Don't open a "top 50 gifts" list. Work from what you already know:

  • Their hobby, upgraded. A better version of something they already use every week.
  • Their consumable. The coffee, tea, hot sauce or bath stuff they'd buy for themselves anyway.
  • Time with you. A planned outing, written on a card, paired with something small to unwrap.
  • A gift card to the place they already go. Not lazy if it's the right place.

For the relative who "has everything," a short list beats a long hunt: pick one category, choose one good thing, stop.

How to avoid Christmas shopping crowds

The simplest way is to not be there. If you're ordering online anyway, the crowds are someone else's Saturday. If a gift truly needs a store:

  • Order ahead for pickup where the store offers it, so the trip is in and out.
  • Go at an off-peak time. NRF's numbers above say Black Friday is the busiest day of the holiday weekend, so pick a different day if you can.
  • Bring the list, not the browsing mood. One store, the items on the list, out the door.

FAQ

Why do so many people hate Christmas shopping?

Mostly the logistics: crowds and parking, register lines, too many choices, not knowing what to get people, and keeping the total inside a budget. The gift-giving part is usually fine; it's the errand part people want to skip.

How can I do my Christmas shopping without going to the store?

Set one budget, collect everyone's wish lists in one place, pick one gift per person from their list, and order online with home or direct-to-recipient delivery. Keep the USPS send-by dates in mind: Dec 17 for Ground Advantage, Dec 18 for Priority Mail and Dec 19 for Priority Mail Express in the lower 48.

How do I avoid crowds when Christmas shopping?

Order online when you can. If a gift needs a store, order ahead for pickup and go at an off-peak time. NRF's 2026 survey found Black Friday is the most popular shopping day of Thanksgiving weekend, so choose another day if you can.

What should I get someone when I have no idea what they want?

Ask them for three to five ideas by a set date. If they don't answer, upgrade something tied to a hobby they already have, buy a consumable they love, plan time together, or get a gift card to a place they already go.

How do I make Christmas shopping easier?

Do it in this order: one total budget, a shorter list (a name draw for adults helps), every wish list in one place, one pick per person, then order from home before the shipping dates.
