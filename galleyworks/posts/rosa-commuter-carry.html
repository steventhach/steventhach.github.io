If It Doesn't Earn Its Bag Space, Don't Gift It

Rosa's commuter rules for crew gifts: a cup caddy, a red key chain and a pilot's Q&A book. Who they're for, who should skip, and what to check first.

By Rosa, a Galley Works editorial voice. Rosa is a character written by our team, not a real crewmember or customer, and has not personally bought, worn or used these products. About our hosts.

My rule is simple: if a gift needs its own bag, it's not a crew gift. On paper, everything here folds, clips or is small enough to tuck away. Quick honesty check: I'm a Galley Works editorial voice, not a real commuter, and I haven't bought or used these. This is what the listings say, and who each pick suits.

Item 01

Roll-aboard drink caddy · GW 002

riemot cup holder: the no-brainer for roll-aboard people

Cleared Who it's for

  • Crew and frequent flyers who walk terminals with a roll-aboard and a coffee
  • Anyone who wants a phone or passport pocket on the handle

Leave it Who should skip it

  • Someone who mostly carries a backpack or duffel with no telescoping handle
  • Anyone who wants a rigid holder; the listing describes soft oxford cloth with hook-and-loop straps

What the listing says

  • Two front pockets for cups or bottles and a back pocket for a phone, passport or boarding pass
  • Attaches with adjustable hook-and-loop straps around most suitcase handles
  • Oxford cloth; machine washable and foldable

Check before you buy

  • Needs a telescoping suitcase handle; it attaches with adjustable hook-and-loop straps
  • Soft oxford cloth rather than a rigid frame, per the listing
  • Machine washable and foldable

Rosa's verdictFolds flat and straps on, per the listing. On paper, it passes the bag-space test.

Remove Before Flight key chain: the Secret Santa that never misses

Cleared Who it's for

  • Pilots, ramp crew and avgeeks who'll recognise it instantly

Leave it Who should skip it
