Item 01

Pilot flight bag · GW 013

Flight Outfitters Lift 2.0: the upgrade they won't buy themselves

Official brand video (YouTube) — not filmed by us. Watch on YouTube

Cleared Who it's for

  • Student and new-hire pilots who carry a headset, tablet and the usual small gear
  • Anyone who wants dedicated pockets instead of one big cavern

Leave it Who should skip it

  • Flight attendants; this bag is designed around pilot gear
  • Someone who already has a flight bag they like, since this is a bigger gift

What the listing says

  • 14 internal pockets, per the listing
  • Padded, fleece-lined headset section and a tablet pocket compatible with iPad
  • Compartments for cords, backup batteries and ADS-B receivers, plus an external radio pocket
  • External pockets for a flashlight, fuel tester and water bottle, a keyring clip and metal hardware

Check before you buy

  • It's a dedicated flight bag rather than a crew roll-aboard, so it's for the pilot on your list
  • Pocket sizes are for pilot gear (headset, tablet, radio), per the listing
  • As a larger gift, it's worth confirming they don't already have a flight bag they like

Dev's verdictIf your pilot still carries a backpack, this is the gift. If they already love their bag, pick the pouch below instead.

Item 02

Tech organizer pouch · GW 009

FYY tech pouch: the thing every new hire forgets

Product review (YouTube) — not filmed by us. Watch on YouTube

Cleared Who it's for

  • Anyone whose chargers, cords and power bank end up loose at the bottom of the bag
  • Commuters and crew who want one grab-and-go kit for the hotel room

Leave it Who should skip it

  • People who carry long cables; the listing says the small mesh pockets suit cords up to about 3 ft
  • Anyone carrying a laptop's worth of tech; at the listed dimensions it's a compact pouch

What the listing says

  • About 7.5 x 4.3 x 2.2 inches, per the listing
  • Water-resistant oxford fabric with a padded interior
  • Double-layer design with 8 compartments, including see-through mesh pockets, and a hand strap
  • Cords and electronics are not included

Check before you buy

  • Listed at about 7.5 x 4.3 x 2.2 inches, so it's a pouch, not a full tech bag
  • The small mesh pockets suit cords up to about 3 ft, per the listing
  • Cords and electronics are not included

Item 03

Classic flying book · GW 015

Stick and Rudder: old book, timeless physics

Product review (YouTube) — not filmed by us. Watch on YouTube

Cleared Who it's for

  • Student pilots and new hires who want to understand why an aircraft does what it does
  • Avgeeks who like a classic

Leave it Who should skip it

  • Someone who wants a modern, illustrated textbook; this is a long-form classic first published in 1944

What the listing says

  • Published by McGraw Hill; about 400 pages
  • Covers angle of attack, how lift is made, why airplanes stall and the landing approach, per the publisher's description

Check before you buy

  • First published in 1944, per the publisher's note on the listing, so the language and examples are of its era
  • About 400 pages from McGraw Hill
  • A book on flying technique, not a guide to airline life

Dev's verdictThe classic on the subject. Pair it with the pouch for a new-hire bundle.
