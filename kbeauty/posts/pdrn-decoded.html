PDRN, Decoded: Read the Label Before the Hype

Salmon DNA, wild ginseng and a 6,000% search spike. Mira reads three of the most-searched PDRN listings line by line: who each is for, who should skip it, and what independent reviewers flag.

By Mira, The Label Skeptic
A Seoul Annotated editorial voice, not a real customer or artist. Mira hasn't used these products; this post is based on listing details and independent reviews. Retrieved Oct 23, 2026.

Verdict before the receipts: PDRN is a real ingredient trend with very real marketing around it. Search interest for 'PDRN serum' grew about 6,000% over five years, per Exploding Topics. That's exactly when labels deserve the closest read. I haven't used any of these; I'm a Seoul Annotated editorial voice, and I'm reading the same listings and independent reviews you can.

Track 01 · Peptide + PDRN serum

medicube PDRN Pink Peptide Serum: the default for a reason, with one caveat

Who it's for
  • First-timers who want the most-searched PDRN serum in a light, everyday texture
  • Combination skin that wants niacinamide and peptides in the same step
Who should skip it
  • Anyone avoiding fragrance: it's on the ingredient list
  • Anyone who wants a stated PDRN amount; the listing doesn't give one

On the listing

  • Salmon DNA PDRN with niacinamide, peptides and adenosine
  • Lightweight serum, listed for all skin types and daily use
  • 1.01 fl oz; also in Rose (vegan) and Ultra Light versions
  • Ingredient list includes fragrance

What independent reviewers report (paraphrased from Glamour, Vogue, Reddit r/koreanskincare)

  • Glamour's testers across three skin types describe a noticeable glow and say it layers without pilling
  • Vogue's reviewer frames it as a hydration and glow step, not a fix for active breakouts
  • Known complaints (r/koreanskincare): a sticky or oily feel for some, a noticeable scent, and occasional small breakouts

Mira's verdict: The easiest way into the trend. Just read the fragrance line before you buy it for sensitive skin.

Track 02 · Plant-based PDRN essence

VT PDRN 100 Essence: read the number twice

Who it's for
  • Vegans who want a PDRN product without fish-derived ingredients
  • Fragrance-avoiders who like a milky, ceramide-rich essence
Who should skip it
  • Anyone buying it for a high DNA dose: independent analysis says DNA is last on the list
  • Oily skin that dislikes rich textures

On the listing

  • States 100,000 ppm PDRN, sourced from Korean wild ginseng
  • Listed as vegan and unscented
  • Milky essence, for morning or night
  • Positioned for people from their 20s to 50s

What independent reviewers report (paraphrased from PDRN Guide, DermApproved)

  • PDRN Guide reads the label: sodium DNA is the last of 31 ingredients, so the 100,000 ppm most plausibly describes the ginseng extract
  • DermApproved describes a fragrance-free, milky essence with a ceramide, niacinamide and adenosine base
  • Known complaints: premium pricing, a texture that oily skin may find rich, and oils that make it unsuitable for fungal-acne-prone routines

Mira's verdict: A pleasant, fragrance-free essence. Buy it for the formula, not for the number on the bottle.

Track 03 · PDRN moisturizer

Anua PDRN Hyaluronic Acid 100 Cream: PDRN where you'd spend anyway

Who it's for
  • Daily makeup wearers: it's listed as non-pilling under makeup
  • Anyone who wants one moisturizer instead of a new serum step
Who should skip it
  • Rich-cream lovers: it's positioned as lightweight
  • Reactive skin: a minority of reviewers report small bumps

On the listing

  • PDRN derived from salmon DNA, with squalane, niacinamide and hyaluronic acid
  • Listed as lightweight, non-sticky and non-comedogenic
  • AM and PM as the last step
  • 2.02 fl oz

What independent reviewers report (paraphrased from Your Insight, Purple Chives)

  • Reviewers describe a light, almost gel texture with a dewy rather than greasy finish
  • Foundation sitting evenly over it is a recurring point
  • Known complaints: not rich enough on its own for very dry skin in winter, and a minority report small bumps or pilling under reapplied sunscreen

Mira's verdict: The sensible way to buy a trend: put it in the step you'd never skip.
