Pads or Bottle? Cut the Step You Don't Need

The internet says you need a toner pad, a calming toner and a snail essence. Jun says pick the one job you actually need. Here's who each is for, who should skip it, and the complaints independent reviewers raise.

By Jun, The Routine Hacker
A Seoul Annotated editorial voice, not a real customer or artist. Jun hasn't used these products; this post is based on listing details and independent reviews. Retrieved Oct 23, 2026.

My rule: two bottles doing one job is a bug. These three get stacked together constantly online, but on paper they do three different jobs: exfoliate, calm, hydrate. Pick one and you've got a routine; pick all three without a plan and you've got a shelf. Quick reminder: I'm a Seoul Annotated editorial voice. I haven't used these; this is the listings and independent reviews, decoded.

Skip track

Stacking a pad, a peel and an acid serum in the same routine.

On repeat

One exfoliating step, used as the label directs, then hydration.

Track 01 · Exfoliating toner pad

medicube Zero Pore Pad 2.0: one jar, two textures, no cotton rounds

Who it's for
  • Oily or combination skin focused on the look of pores and shine
  • Anyone who wants exfoliation and toning in a single swipe
Who should skip it
  • Very reactive skin
  • Anyone already using another acid product: it lists lactic and salicylic acid

On the listing

  • 70 dual-sided pads: embossed side, then silky side
  • Lists lactic acid, salicylic acid, white willow bark and citric acid
  • Lavender among the botanicals
  • Directions: swipe embossed side, finish with silky side, pat in

What independent reviewers report (paraphrased from Tikevo, Corea Skincare)

  • Reviewers describe smoother texture and makeup sitting better, with pores looking less obvious rather than shrinking
  • Tikevo notes the sebum and 'pore waste' percentages are brand-study numbers
  • Known complaints: alcohol in the formula can sting sensitive or dry skin; twice-daily use made one reviewer's skin tight, so both suggest a slower start

Jun's verdict: Your exfoliating step, full stop. If you buy this, don't buy another acid.

Skin that looks red or feels warm and wants a calm, simple toner

Anyone wanting exfoliation: this is a calming toner, not an acid

Mira: One hero ingredient at a stated percentage, no fragrance. That's how a label earns trust.

See it on Amazon↗

Track 02 · Calming toner

Anua Heartleaf 77: the calm step, not the scrub step

Who it's for
  • Skin that looks red or feels warm and wants a simple toner
  • Fragrance-avoiders: it's listed as fragrance-free
Who should skip it
  • Anyone hoping for exfoliation; this is a calming toner
  • Dry skin that wants a cushioning toner: reviewers call it watery

On the listing

  • 77% heartleaf (houttuynia cordata) extract plus hyaluronic acid
  • Listed as fragrance-free and non-comedogenic
  • Layer 2-3 times, or soak pads for a 3-5 minute mask
  • 8.45 fl oz

What independent reviewers report (paraphrased from The Dew Journal, ELLE)

  • The Dew Journal describes a watery, fragrance-free step that rarely stings, at its best on red or reactive skin
  • ELLE's editor pick praises how it calms the look of stressed skin
  • Known complaints: many users say it feels like water and changes nothing visible; ELLE notes it may not suit very sensitive skin

Jun's verdict: Pick this or the pad, not both on the same night. Calm and scrub are different jobs.

Track 03 · Hydrating essence

COSRX Snail 96 Mucin: the benchmark hydration layer

Who it's for
  • Dry or dull-looking skin that wants one affordable hydration step
  • Anyone building a routine from zero
Who should skip it
  • Vegans: it's snail secretion filtrate
  • Anyone who hates a slippery texture

On the listing

  • 96.3% snail secretion filtrate
  • Also lists sodium hyaluronate, allantoin, panthenol and betaine
  • The listing recommends a patch test before face use
  • 3.38 fl oz

What independent reviewers report (paraphrased from Cosmopolitan)

  • Cosmopolitan's reviewer describes it as very hydrating with a dewier look
  • The same review calls it affordable for the category
  • Known complaints: a slimy texture that takes getting used to, a slight residue, and it isn't vegan

Jun's verdict: If you only add one thing, add hydration. This is the long-running default.
